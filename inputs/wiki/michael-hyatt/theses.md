# Michael Hyatt — Recurring Investment Theses

## Direct company investing

- **Explicit — founder-first, pivot-aware underwriting:** Hyatt prioritizes the entrepreneur over the initial plan, expecting major pivots and testing whether founders listen, adapt, and execute. [ev:michael-hyatt-0321] [ev:michael-hyatt-0169] [ev:michael-hyatt-0170]

- **Explicit — relationship capital:** A direct investment is a decade-long working relationship; personal affinity, character, trust, and constructive disagreement are threshold considerations. [ev:michael-hyatt-0032] [ev:michael-hyatt-0033] [ev:michael-hyatt-0193] [ev:michael-hyatt-0532]

- **Explicit — B2B preference:** He prefers B2B businesses because enterprise customers can support larger transactions, while capital-intensive consumer scaling creates concern. [ev:michael-hyatt-0009] [ev:michael-hyatt-0010] [ev:michael-hyatt-0172]

- **Explicit — customer budget is truth:** Paying, referenceable customers, repeat purchases, expansion, and negative churn de-risk the company more than founder or investor conviction. [ev:michael-hyatt-0006] [ev:michael-hyatt-0156] [ev:michael-hyatt-0158] [ev:michael-hyatt-0436]

- **Explicit — large market plus product-market fit:** He wants attractive, substantial markets and has said product-market fit in a large, expanding market can outweigh team weaknesses; elsewhere he says a bad market can defeat excellent management. [ev:michael-hyatt-0068] [ev:michael-hyatt-0221] [ev:michael-hyatt-0397]

- **Explicit — defensibility through discontinuous value:** Scalable, defensible companies with strong IP, sticky niches, or roughly tenfold improvement are preferable to incremental products or superficial AI wrappers. [ev:michael-hyatt-0313] [ev:michael-hyatt-0072] [ev:michael-hyatt-0376] [ev:michael-hyatt-0380]

- **Explicit — economics before narrative:** High gross margin, recurring revenue, credible net profitability, and sound unit economics matter more than top-line growth purchased through losses or discounting. [ev:michael-hyatt-0314] [ev:michael-hyatt-0075] [ev:michael-hyatt-0423] [ev:michael-hyatt-0432]

- **Explicit — software margin benchmark:** He has used at least 70% gross margin as an adequacy benchmark for software and the 80% range as best-in-class; this does not establish the same gate for non-software businesses. [ev:michael-hyatt-0174]

- **Explicit — restrained financing:** Capital is attractive when it creates incremental revenue, enterprise value, or better margins; unnecessary rounds dilute founders and can create dependence on future financing. [ev:michael-hyatt-0271] [ev:michael-hyatt-0272] [ev:michael-hyatt-0427] [ev:michael-hyatt-0431]

- **Explicit — entry price and terms drive returns:** Operating progress must justify valuation, ordinary exit outcomes must support the price, and control rights or preferences can matter more than the headline valuation. [ev:michael-hyatt-0067] [ev:michael-hyatt-0073] [ev:michael-hyatt-0163] [ev:michael-hyatt-0429]

- **Explicit — active ownership must earn its burden:** He concentrates on a few companies where operating help can materially affect outcomes and evaluates the required involvement against expected upside and downside. [ev:michael-hyatt-0069] [ev:michael-hyatt-0070] [ev:michael-hyatt-0404] [ev:michael-hyatt-0407]

- **Inferred — founder-originated problems:** His repeated advice to begin with a personally experienced problem suggests a preference for authentic founder-market insight, but only some statements frame it as a direct investment criterion. [ev:michael-hyatt-0175] [ev:michael-hyatt-0312] [ev:michael-hyatt-0449]

- **Inferred — focused, complementary execution:** Operating advice suggests he favors teams that divide responsibilities by strength, hire superior people, delegate authority, and avoid distracting initiatives. [ev:michael-hyatt-0188] [ev:michael-hyatt-0216] [ev:michael-hyatt-0287] [ev:michael-hyatt-0437]

## Technology themes and evolution

- **Security, cloud, and infrastructure:** He has expressed a favorable long-term view of security, cloud, AI, network automation, and indispensable infrastructure; DataStealth is the clearest operating expression of the security thesis. [ev:michael-hyatt-0041] [ev:michael-hyatt-0104] [ev:michael-hyatt-0304] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0151]

- **AI—bullish technology, skeptical deal market:** He expects AI to transform occupations and believes the market underestimates its significance, while warning about bubble valuations, crowding, hallucinations, gimmicky entrants, and products without customer utility. [ev:michael-hyatt-0025] [ev:michael-hyatt-0240] [ev:michael-hyatt-0059] [ev:michael-hyatt-0243] [ev:michael-hyatt-0417]

- **Source-specific AI maturity views:** In the June 2023 discussion he expected another one or two years of market development and dismissed many products as demonstrations; other evidence remains bullish on profound progress by 2030 but still rejects AI as sufficiently reliable for family-office stock selection. [ctx:michael-hyatt-0254] [ev:michael-hyatt-0377] [ev:michael-hyatt-0379] [ev:michael-hyatt-0241] [ev:michael-hyatt-0411]

- **Quantum and superconductors:** He expects quantum to become significant but has also called the market premature; his support for Meissner treats superconductors as enabling infrastructure for the future quantum ecosystem. [ev:michael-hyatt-0446] [ev:michael-hyatt-0329] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0161]

- **Broader forecasts, not confirmed mandates:** Autonomous transport, batteries, fintech, manufactured organs, gene editing, and technology applied across industries recur as opportunity observations, but do not establish direct portfolio allocation. [ev:michael-hyatt-0047] [ev:michael-hyatt-0048] [ev:michael-hyatt-0049] [ev:michael-hyatt-0050] [ev:michael-hyatt-0142] [ev:michael-hyatt-0212]

## Fund allocation and private markets

- **Explicit — selective LP exposure:** The family office invests through venture, private-equity, and fund-of-funds structures, while Hyatt benchmarks invested funds against market quartiles and favors managers with meaningful personal capital at risk. [ctx:michael-hyatt-0009] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0016] [ev:michael-hyatt-0251] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0236]

- **Explicit — skeptical of venture as a standalone asset class:** Venture depends heavily on rare outliers, has a high normal failure rate, and in his view rarely beats public equities consistently enough for the risk. [ev:michael-hyatt-0231] [ev:michael-hyatt-0233] [ev:michael-hyatt-0234] [ev:michael-hyatt-0245]

- **Explicit — preference for repeatable private-equity outcomes:** He prefers private equity’s more repeatable three-to-four-times target and requires an illiquidity and effort premium over public markets. [ev:michael-hyatt-0236] [ev:michael-hyatt-0467] [ev:michael-hyatt-0469]

- **Explicit — Northleaf differs from angel investing:** In the Northleaf context he favors larger, later-stage, profitable companies; Northleaf reported that his advisory responsibilities include sourcing and evaluating venture and growth opportunities. [ev:michael-hyatt-0230] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0177]

## Public markets and cryptocurrency

- **Public equities:** The family office uses buy-and-hold rather than active trading, options, or short selling; reported implementation has centered on broad Vanguard ETFs plus selected large technology stocks. [ev:michael-hyatt-0412] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0233] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0234]

- **Public-market discipline:** He says he avoids market timing and does not consider present AI reliable enough to make stock-selection decisions. [ev:michael-hyatt-0414] [ev:michael-hyatt-0411]

- **Cryptocurrency tension across sources:** He reported owning Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Polymath and working with Polymath; in another statement he supported capped Bitcoin exposure through an ETF at no more than 5% of liquid net worth, while describing cryptocurrency or Bitcoin as speculative or tactical rather than a productive long-term asset. [ctx:michael-hyatt-0122] [ev:michael-hyatt-0237] [ev:michael-hyatt-0277] [ev:michael-hyatt-0549]

## Real estate and operating-company strategy

- **Real estate:** Reported family-office allocation has included income-producing real estate and debt structures, including fund exposure to commercial properties in secondary cities; this is distinct from direct technology investing. [ctx:michael-hyatt-0578] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0147] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0235]

- **Operating-company ownership:** He prefers control or majority positions when substantial intervention may be necessary and dislikes powerless minority ownership during deterioration. DataStealth represents a majority acquisition and return to operations, not an ordinary angel position. [ev:michael-hyatt-0232] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0151] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0154]

- **Portfolio construction differences:** A 2022 report described equal allocations to stocks, real estate, and venture/private equity; another first-person account described broad thirds of public equities, private equity with less venture, and acquisitions or major direct investments. The difference may reflect evolution or category relabeling rather than a fixed permanent policy. [ctx:michael-hyatt-0145] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0101] [ev:michael-hyatt-0413]
