# Sarah Downey — Investment Memory

This memory covers the resolved Sarah Downey associated with Accomplice, not same-name individuals. It synthesizes attributed interviews, talks, and first-person writing with source-reported context. Much of the source material lacks publication dates, several transcripts are automated or duplicated, and some media had no transcript; statements that sound current should therefore be read as of their source, not as present-day facts. Direct criteria are separated from operating lessons whose investment implications are marked **Inferred**. Firm policy, personal angel practice, Yubari activity, example Spearhead filters, and broader observations about venture capital are not interchangeable.

## founder_team

People dominate Downey’s earliest-stage underwriting because there may be little beyond an idea and an unfinished product; she explicitly ranks people ahead of market opportunity in angel investing. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0006] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0314] Her recurring questions are: Who are the founders, why are they building this, what motivates them, how hard do they work, and how do they interact with one another? [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0007] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0013] She looks for trustworthiness, grit, urgency, honesty, adaptability, dedication, team-oriented selflessness, curiosity, and mission orientation. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0191] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0303] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0370]

Her strongest positive signal is exceptional talent known firsthand: the former coworker who repeatedly appeared brilliant, resourceful, and likely to make something work. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0033] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0034] A trusted referral is the next-best signal, with recommendations discounted as they move farther from direct trusted relationships. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0220] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0222] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0223] Pedigree, appearance, conventional degrees, and prior founding experience are secondary to being exceptional, talented, and compelling. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0319]

She wants every founder to bring an unusually strong, differentiated capability, and the team collectively to cover the skills essential to the business. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0304] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0237] Technical companies should have a technical founder or already-hired senior engineering leader; academic spinouts often need business strength to complement excellent technology. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0131] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0045] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0046] Redundant groups of nontechnical founders with inflated, poorly differentiated titles are a warning; unique contribution matters more than title. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0022] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0023] Founder type must fit the job: she has observed operator-oriented B2B founders struggle with visionary consumer plays, while relevant professional depth and an operator orientation may suit legal automation. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0138] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0141]

Motivation is unusually important. Founders should care deeply about the problem—ideally from experience—and love what they are putting into the world; money or ego as the primary motive is a major negative because it may not sustain them through adversity. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0065] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0069] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0238] Full-time commitment is expected by the time founders ask VCs to commit. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0132] Her poor-investment lessons reinforce this hierarchy: she regretted overlooking ego or questionable commitment because a product or market looked strong. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0072] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0193]

Trust boundaries are hard. Substantive lying, fabricated term sheets, or false metrics puts the investment out; the long founder-investor relationship depends on trust. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0128] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0130] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0074] Sexist or disrespectful conduct in a pitch is also categorical grounds not to invest, and she believes investors should screen out founders likely to harm employees. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0206] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0071] Ego-driven leadership is additionally problematic because founders set workplace conditions and owe employees a safe, welcoming environment. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0070]

She tests communication and self-knowledge, not polish alone. She prefers concise stories and point-first outreach that earns a second meeting; rambling first contact suggests poor communication, persuasion, judgment, emotional intelligence, or self-regulation. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0010] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0011] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0126] She asks candidates to explain something they know deeply or discuss a passion, observing off-the-cuff thinking, teaching ability, values, and authentic enthusiasm. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0366] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0367] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0369] She also attends to body language and uses the Enneagram as one interpretive tool, not as a stated pass/fail test. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0315] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0287]

Intellectual honesty and coachability matter: founders should distinguish genuine wins from weak metrics, articulate what is hard, listen without defensiveness, and ask for help rather than papering over problems. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0018] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0061] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0062] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0311] Prior failure is acceptable; Downey says investors repeatedly back founders who failed with integrity because adaptability and character matter more than a spotless startup record. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0295] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0296]

## market_opportunity

The opportunity must plausibly become very large, with the needed scale varying with fund size. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0014] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0036] Downey’s venture logic seeks massive outcomes and highly asymmetric returns; a good, sustainable company can still be non-venture-scale and may be better bootstrapped. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0232] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0233] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0301] Her stated Accomplice-era floor was a plausible company value at least comparable to a $200 million fund, but this was fund-specific rather than a universal threshold. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0282]

The customer problem should be important to a substantial group and preferably essential—a “painkiller,” not a merely pleasant feature. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0058] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0275] She independently assesses addressable market rather than accepting founder assertions. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0280] **Inferred:** Her operating advice favors early customer conversations and even small payments as strong validation of need and willingness to pay, but does not establish revenue as a universal prerequisite. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0009] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0356]

She is attracted to bold visions that materially change or save lives, exemplified by Ezra’s effort to make early cancer detection more affordable and accessible; incremental improvements to routine business software are less personally motivating. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0001] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0002] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0003] She also favors category creators over companies battling for a foothold in already crowded spaces. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0271]

## competition_defensibility

Founders should know competitors without obsessively copying them or dismissing formidable incumbents. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0020] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0021] Claiming there is no competition signals either inadequate research or unjustified confidence; the market, not the founder, decides superiority. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0122]

She prefers technology that is difficult to copy and preserves pricing or positioning power; patents may help but are not required. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0276] Easily replicated products and markets requiring expensive battles for users, visibility, advertising, or share are negative. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0325] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0326] Portfolio conflicts can independently prevent investment when a company is too close to an existing holding. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0244]

## product_solution

The product must demonstrably do what is claimed; where Downey lacks the technical expertise, she recommends independent expert validation. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0251] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0281] **Inferred:** She favors getting products into users’ hands, combining stated feedback with observed behavior, and treating users as development partners rather than relying only on founder intuition. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0148] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0149] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0150]

Her frontier-product taste favors genuinely new interaction models rather than porting an existing experience into a new medium with minimal change. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0175] In immersive technology, she has emphasized accessible creation tools, middleware, streaming efficiency, 3D creation infrastructure, 3D audio, and privacy-preserving experiences. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0043] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0112] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0114] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0115] Privacy by design means collecting little data, using non-personally identifiable structures, and preserving an enjoyable, simple experience. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0102] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0105] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0106]

## traction_growth

Traction is stage- and company-specific, not one universal numerical gate. Relevant signals may include product milestones, users, downloads, paying customers, revenue, partnerships, engagement, acquisition efficiency, network effects, pipeline, lifetime value, or defensibility. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0252] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0358] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0359] Pre-seed can have customer validation without significant revenue, while seed should show some product or team de-risking. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0356] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0357]

Downey can accept pre-revenue risk when user adoption exists, arguing that monetization may follow users. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0259] She does not expect linear development: strong companies can experience internal rifts, pivots, near-insolvency, or strategic resets. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0260] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0090] Yet founders should connect the amount raised and spending plan to milestones and time, rather than relying on unsupported long-range projections that she considers unknowable at the earliest stage. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0306] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0012]

## business_model_economics

The venture model should support revenue growth with low incremental cost; labor-intensive studios and high-touch services are less attractive because headcount rises with output. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0278] Founders should understand their economics, target customer, pricing, and acquisition plan. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0308] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0338] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0339]

Downey is skeptical of claims that undifferentiated collected data will later become a valuable product, particularly because indiscriminate data monetization conflicts with privacy. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0016] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0293] She is also wary of hit-driven content economics, including VR content and pure gaming titles, despite her personal enthusiasm for gaming. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0111] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0336]

**Inferred:** Concentrated dependencies deserve diligence: one dominant customer, one holder of irreplaceable knowledge, one critical supplier, or one investor can create operational, pricing, control, or financing risk. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0142] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0143] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0144] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0145] These are financing-risk warnings, not blanket refusal rules.

## deal_terms_valuation

Company selection dominates terms in outlier investing; Downey says terms and paperwork matter most in losing or middling outcomes. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0180] Still, she treats absurdly high valuation as evidence of poor market judgment, bad advice, or manipulation, and absurdly low valuation as a possible signal of weak judgment or quality. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0123] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0124] A high valuation also reduces ownership and raises the hurdle for the next round, while an excessively low valuation can leave founders insufficiently motivated after dilution. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0245] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0313]

She views early valuation as more art than science. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0167] Convertible debt can avoid premature pricing and be simpler and cheaper than a priced round; SAFEs also eliminate interest calculations. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0166] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0031] She generally regards uncapped notes as unfavorable for angels, while valuing caps and pro-rata rights that preserve ownership in winners. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0170] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0032] Pro rata has a practical constraint: maintaining ownership becomes expensive as a winner’s valuation rises. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0171]

Crisis financing can force a genuine tradeoff between accepting worsened terms for certainty and defending company value; she explicitly says there is no simple answer. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0041] Corporate strategic investors may add complications because their incentives extend beyond financial return. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0335]

## timing

She explicitly asks whether now is the right moment to build. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0179] Being too early can mean years of R&D, absent demand, and repeated financing before customers are ready; being too late means saturation, diminished novelty, and limited room for a new entrant. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0273] Rising category investment and acquisition interest is a favorable signal, but not a substitute for customer readiness. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0274]

Her historical AR/VR view combined optimism about an inflection point with caution about premature subcategories. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0052] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0053] For example, she judged realistic haptics strategically important but too early because adoption, technology, and integration ecosystems were immature. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0374] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0376] During the coronavirus crisis, she saw new needs in scalable disease testing and gaps for angels to fund, while saying VCs had a stronger immediate duty to protect existing companies. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0037] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0038] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0039]

## investor_fit_constraints

Her strongest direct fit is very early-stage, risky technology: pre-seed and small seed personally, and historically pre-seed through Series A in her early-stage VC framing. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0008] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0320] [ctx:sarah-downey-accomplice-0191] Accomplice’s source-era target was $1 million–$2.5 million initially for 10%–20%; separately, Yubari reported $10,000–$100,000 pre-seed checks, so these mandates must not be blended. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0242] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0298] As of January 2020, she said she had shifted to an Accomplice operating-partner/contractor role and was no longer writing checks for the firm at that time, while continuing boards, company work, Rev Boston, and Spearhead activities. [ctx:sarah-downey-accomplice-0172]

The strongest thematic fit is frontier technology that feels like science fiction: robotics, AI, AR/VR, gaming-related infrastructure, identity, privacy, and future consumer health. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0159] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0202] Historical AR priorities included social, fitness, and workplace productivity; within VR she favored enabling software over capital-intensive headsets. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0055] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0110] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0112] She explicitly solicited realistic haptics, private virtual environments, and universal bot-building infrastructure. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0378] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0104] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0157]

Out preferences include weak founder conviction, lack of personal conviction in the space, dishonesty, disrespect, missing critical skills, non-venture scale, crowded or easily copied products, implausible go-to-market, poor economics, and capital needs mismatched to the fund. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0256] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0239] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0237] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0331] Her categorical headset statement was based on extreme capital intensity, while broader warnings about hardware, cybersecurity, medical-device approval, or supply chains are risk and fit concerns rather than universal exclusions. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0110] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0241] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0277]

Investor-founder chemistry is mutual: she invests in people she likes, seeks values and personality alignment, and advises founders to choose investors who contribute beyond money. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0116] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0194] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0077] She expects angels to understand and care about a space rather than making founders educate them. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0035] Her preferred investor posture combines empathy, expertise, introductions, hiring and fundraising help with restraint on day-to-day operations; the earlier the company, the more involvement may be needed. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0097] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0266] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0360]

A tentative, **Inferred** diligence sequence is: establish exceptional and trustworthy founders; test authentic motivation, commitment, complementary skills, and coachability; independently size the market and timing; verify the product and differentiation; inspect adoption appropriate to stage; examine economics, go-to-market, capital intensity, and concentrated dependencies; then assess valuation, terms, portfolio conflict, and mutual fit. This sequence synthesizes her stated ordering and questions but is not a published scoring model. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0279] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0314] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0281]

## Distinctive / doesn't-fit-the-taxonomy

Downey’s decision style is unusually personal and intuition-forward: she describes authenticity and autonomy as core values, reads body language for hidden motivation, and prefers being wrong while acting consistently with her own instincts over copying another investor’s standards. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0119] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0315] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0195] This is balanced—not replaced—by research, questioning, technical audits, and concise synthesis. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0355] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0281]

Her framework contains an explicit exception that resists rigid taxonomies: she says exceptional founders can override almost every ordinary pass reason. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0231] That exception should not erase her hard ethical boundaries around lying, sexism, or harmful leadership. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0130] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0206] She also rejects paid pitch access and reports never sourcing an investment from a pitch event, preferring trusted referrals or thoughtful direct outreach. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0087] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0088] [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0089] Finally, remote work is not a negative screen, reflecting an explicit evolution after the pandemic rather than a location-based preference. [ev:sarah-downey-accomplice-0017]
