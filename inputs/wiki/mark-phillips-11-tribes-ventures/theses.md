# Mark Phillips — Recurring Investment Theses

## Direct venture investment

- **Explicit — founder and company outcomes are co-equal design objectives.** 11 Tribes intentionally evaluates founder wellbeing and development alongside commercial performance and finances founder-resilience support with each investment. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0008] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0157] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0158]

- **Explicit — people are a source of both upside and risk.** Phillips puts exceptional weight on founder character, motivation, market fit, leadership, and team composition; the firm uses psychometric profiles and regards co-founder conflict as an execution risk. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0095] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0213] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0085] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0395]

- **Explicit — sustainable, durable, profitable growth beats growth at any cost.** The desired company occupies a middle ground between a lifestyle business and a hypergrowth company forced into repeated, highly dilutive financing. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0135] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0223] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0320]

- **Explicit — disciplined entry price is part of risk control.** Non-coastal seed markets are attractive partly because lower valuations preserve upside and financing options; fashionable AI pricing is a particular concern. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0163] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0254] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0359]

- **Explicit — capital efficiency permits a different venture model.** Phillips wants companies that can reach breakeven with moderate seed financing and can scale without depending on continuous rounds. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0124] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0184] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0326]

- **Explicit — moderate exits can produce strong fund returns.** The fund underwrites approximately 10x growth while accepting plausible sub-unicorn outcomes, including modeled $100–$150 million exits and, in some cases, a $50 million outcome sufficient for targeted cash returns. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0134] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0219] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0249]

- **Explicit — bounded niches can be venture-backable.** A small market can work when pain is acute, incumbency is weak, attainable share is high, and the resulting company value fits the fund model. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0185] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0329]

- **Explicit — domain expertise creates a right to win.** The preferred founder often comes directly from an industry, understands an operational problem intimately, and can reach customers efficiently. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0126] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0253]

- **Explicit — distribution and workflow depth are replacing code as moats.** As software becomes easier to build, defensibility moves toward customer access, embedded workflow, community, shared identity, and learning systems. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0244] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0245] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0293]

- **Explicit — vertical AI, not generic AI exposure.** AI should create a previously unattainable improvement in customer experience, retention, or go-to-market. Generic wrappers and foundation-model businesses are excluded under the stated posture because their moats and economics appear weak. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0131] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0132] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0192]

- **Explicit — genuine demand outranks product polish.** Customer urgency, signed contracts, and revenue demand can justify underwriting product immaturity; polished technology without commercial demand is less attractive. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0168] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0187]

- **Inferred — founder resilience is treated as an investable operating capability.** His operating advice favors self-awareness, restorative practices, candid feedback, and dimensions of identity beyond company value, but these should not be converted into universal prerequisites. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0092] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0270] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0292]

- **Inferred — focused execution and customer discovery are positive selection signals.** Phillips admires direct observation of customer pain, incorporating feedback, disciplined prioritization, and action under uncertainty; most of this evidence is operating advice rather than a stated gate. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0109] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0110] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0265]

## Evolution and tensions

- **Stage evolution:** Fund I included pre-seed exposure and typical $250,000–$300,000 checks, while Fund II was described as exclusively post-revenue with $1 million–$1.5 million checks; NQS demonstrates that contracted demand and experienced founders can support a deliberate exception. [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0065] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0069] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0127] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0168]

- **Sector evolution:** the early post-COVID thesis emphasized distributed work, education, agriculture, sustainability, and traceability; later positioning emphasizes niche vertical AI and blue-collar industries while remaining generalist. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0201] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0082] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0140]

- **Leadership evolution:** earlier accounts emphasize minority positions and syndication; a later first-party account says the firm shifted toward leading and board influence. The later primary statement outweighs an aggregator claim that 11 Tribes does not lead. [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0016] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0115] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0138] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0325]

- **Market-size tension:** Phillips can accept a $250 million niche, but only when attainable share and exit value fit the return model; he does not claim every small market is venture-backable. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0143] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0180]

- **Capital-intensity tension:** substantial outside financing is a risk, not an automatic refusal. His preference remains for moderate financing, but he explicitly did not treat capital need alone as disqualifying in one evaluated venture. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0072] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0362]

## Fund allocation strategy

- **Fund I:** reported as an $11 million, 2021-vintage fund, with approximately 45–50% intended for initial investments and 55–60% reserved for follow-ons. [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0040] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0043] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0106]

- **Fund II:** first-party reporting gives a March 2025 close at approximately $46 million–$46.4 million; the planned deployment was $1 million–$1.5 million checks across roughly 25–30 companies over two to three years. [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0137] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0334] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0127]

- **Portfolio construction:** the strategy favors a comparatively high success rate and moderate exits rather than a portfolio dependent entirely on rare unicorns. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0061] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0159]

- **Ownership and reserves:** the fund generally seeks approximately 5–7% ownership plus pro rata and reserves capital to increase exposure to demonstrated winners. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0104] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0376]

- **Founder-development allocation:** 2% of investable capital is reported as reserved non-dilutively for coaching, counseling, leadership development, therapy, and organizational health. [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0096]

- **LP network as operating leverage:** many Fund I LPs were current or former operators, and Phillips treats the LP base as a source of practical help for portfolio companies. [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0090] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0288]

## Public markets, real estate, and operating-company strategy

The supplied evidence establishes no public-equity strategy, public-market portfolio, public-securities mandate, real-estate thesis, real-estate allocation, or real-estate holding. This is a source-coverage limitation, not evidence that Phillips has never made such investments. The affirmative record consistently concerns early-stage private venture investing. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0209]

- **Operating-company strategy:** Phillips favors sustainable profitability, deliberate ownership structure, owner-independent earnings, focused execution, and explicit accountability. Applied to investment selection, the focus and leadership portions remain **Inferred** unless separately stated as criteria.
