# Mark Phillips — Investment Memory

**Scope and source limitations.** This memory covers the resolved Mark Phillips, founder/managing partner of 11 Tribes Ventures; it excludes same-name people. It synthesizes attributed speech and source-reported context, not independently verified holdings or performance. Several transcripts are undated, repeated pages may reflect the same interview, and some company names are uncertain transcript renderings. Current-sounding claims should be read as of their source date. Strategy figures also evolved between Fund I and Fund II rather than forming one timeless mandate.

## founder_team

Phillips is unusually founder-centered: he says 11 Tribes evaluates founder outcomes alongside business outcomes and regards investment in people as part of the return strategy, not merely a benefit layered onto it. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0210] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0212]

- **In:** founders for whom he can develop strong conviction about motivation, market fit, and ability to bring the product to market. He places unusually high weight on the person and believes an exceptional founder can sometimes overcome weaknesses in the initial idea or market. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0213] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0214] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0369]

- **In:** integrity, accountability, humility, drive, judgment, and service-oriented leadership. His formulations include “humble, hungry, and smart,” while other evidence emphasizes leaders who serve employees and attend to their holistic welfare. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0025] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0026] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0430]

- **In:** intrinsic commitment to a meaningful problem. He actively investigates whether the founder is motivated by the problem and its beneficial impact rather than primarily by money; financial motivation is a negative durability signal because it may not persist through adversity. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0033] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0034] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0393]

- **In:** operator-founders with deep industry experience and a distinctive “right to win.” He links domain knowledge to faster customer understanding, more efficient problem-solving, and commercial execution; Fund II reportedly showed a favorable pattern of first-time founders emerging from industry. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0051] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0059] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0058]

- **In:** complementary teams rather than one universal founder archetype. The firm uses psychometric profiles to assess team composition, and it evaluates reactions to criticism as a tentative signal of coachability and constructive conflict. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0085] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0176] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0177]

- **In:** focused, hands-on ownership, evidence-based hard decisions, and accountability for chosen commitments. A founder’s willingness to make difficult personnel or operating decisions can support follow-on conviction. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0215] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0186] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0278]

- **Out or serious concern:** dishonest or inflated representations, destructive co-founder dynamics, unwillingness to receive candid feedback, and motivation fundamentally mismatched with the problem. Overstating recurring revenue is an inferred credibility problem, while the fund explicitly treats founder conflict and organizational-health risks as investable execution risks. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0084] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0045] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0299]

- **Boundary:** wellbeing is not shorthand for low standards. Phillips favors demanding expectations combined with strong support, explicit commitments, timelines, and accountability. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0275] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0300] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0301]

**Inferred, tentative diligence questions:** Why are you personally committed to this problem? What industry experience gives you a distinctive right to win? How do the founders handle conflict and critical feedback? Which difficult evidence-based decision have you made? What practices allow you and your family to remain durable through adversity? These questions synthesize stated criteria and operating advice rather than reproduce a published checklist. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0033] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0126] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0178] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0270]

## market_opportunity

Phillips does not treat a conventionally huge TAM as the only route to venture returns. He is willing to back a bounded niche when customer pain is acute, the incumbent is weak, competitive attention is limited, and the attainable outcome fits the fund’s economics. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0185] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0329]

- **In:** expanding industries, category creation, and markets where technology or customer behavior is shifting. He places more weight on future change and the ability to acquire customers than on perfect present-day TAM calculations. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0101] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0112] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0114]

- **In:** narrow, overlooked markets in which focused execution could capture a large share. NQS was attractive despite an approximately $250 million market because the pain appeared acute, the incumbent weak, and the company narrowly focused. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0143] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0185]

- **In:** non-coastal North American ecosystems—especially tier-two and tier-three markets—where founder quality may be comparable but entry pricing lower. Sources name the Midwest, Southeast, South, Mid-Atlantic, mountain region, Austin, and Denver, showing that “non-coastal” is a pricing and access thesis rather than a literal regional formula. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0122] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0123] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0139]

- **Historical themes:** in 2021, preferred areas included distributed work, virtual education/edtech, agriculture/agtech, sustainability, and food traceability. Later evidence adds blue-collar niches and vertical AI while preserving a generalist or sector-diversified posture. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0201] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0082] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0165]

- **Boundary:** a small market is not automatically attractive. It must still support the fund’s required return; NQS prompted explicit debate over whether its market and pre-revenue execution risk were underwritable. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0180]

**Inferred, tentative questions:** How acute is the customer pain? Why has the incumbent failed to solve it? Can a focused entrant capture meaningful share before larger competitors care? Does the plausible outcome fit 11 Tribes’ fund model without assuming a multibillion-dollar company? [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0185] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0251] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0252]

## competition_defensibility

Phillips increasingly treats product code alone as a weak moat because falling development barriers make software easier to copy. In AI, he looks instead for distribution, workflow integration, community, shared identity, and superior systems for generating and learning from data. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0139] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0245] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0293]

- **In:** distinctive niches or channel partnerships that lower acquisition cost and accelerate access to customers. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0069] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0142]

- **In:** a customer community or shared identity that becomes difficult for competitors to reproduce. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0068]

- **In:** workflow-level integration and accumulated learning systems rather than mere possession of data or an AI label. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0245] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0293]

- **Conditional:** CPG and hardware are investable when there is a compelling product and credible right to win; they are not excluded simply because the broader portfolio is technology-heavy. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0054] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0130]

- **Out:** AI wrappers and foundational LLM businesses under the Fund II posture, because he expects weak differentiation, commoditization, and deteriorating economics. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0131]

- **Concern:** fashionable, heavily competed AI rounds where investor enthusiasm inflates price; he expects frontier labs to compete hardest in markets capable of multibillion-dollar outcomes. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0357] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0251]

**Inferred, tentative questions:** If the product can be rebuilt, what remains defensible? Who owns customer access? How embedded is the company in workflow? Does usage generate a learning advantage, community, or switching cost? [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0411] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0412] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0293]

## product_solution

Phillips prefers solutions derived from direct operational pain and refined through customer feedback. This is strongest as operating evidence rather than a universal gate, but it aligns with his explicit preference for industry-experienced founders. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0109] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0110] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0402]

- **In:** products with clear customer value, acute demand, and a focused wedge that can expand into adjacent workflows. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0220] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0190]

- **In:** vertical AI that produces a previously unattainable improvement in go-to-market, customer experience, or retention—not basic AI adoption presented as differentiation. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0132] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0192]

- **Strong validation, not an absolute prerequisite:** demonstrated customer urgency can matter more than polish. He prefers real revenue demand despite product problems over a polished product without commercial pull, and signed contracts can validate a pre-revenue company. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0187] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0168]

- **AI economics and safeguards:** he wants token-level spending tied to demonstrable value creation and advises protection of company data and guardrails while formal governance catches up. The latter is operating advice, not a stated automatic rejection rule. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0255] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0256]

## traction_growth

The recurring Fund II strike zone is post-revenue seed: approximately $500,000–$2 million of revenue, with realized recurring revenue preferred over annualized run-rate claims. Other sources state an upper bound of $3 million, showing a range rather than a single immutable cutoff. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0129] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0075]

- **In:** demonstrated significant growth that venture capital can accelerate rather than replace. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0226] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0445]

- **Exception:** although Fund II was described as exclusively post-revenue under then-current conditions, experienced founders, predictable contracted pipeline, and signed customer contracts can justify pre-revenue underwriting. NQS is the principal example, not proof that traction is generally unnecessary. [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0124] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0168] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0182]

- **In:** substantive financial models at every stage, with assumptions and controllable metrics tracked. Failure to do so reduces confidence in the trajectory. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0174] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0175]

- **In for follow-ons:** rapid growth and demand-driven capital needs can warrant increasing ownership; the firm reserves capacity for companies demonstrating winning trajectories. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0189] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0376]

## business_model_economics

The core model is sustainable, durable, profitable growth rather than growth at any cost. Phillips seeks the middle ground between a lifestyle business and a capital-intensive hypergrowth startup. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0065] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0135]

- **In:** sound unit economics, a credible path to profitability, high-quality earnings, and results that persist without dependence on the owner’s labor or personal relationships. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0218] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0431] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0429]

- **In:** capital-efficient companies able to use a moderate seed round to approach cash-flow breakeven and retain financing optionality. This preference is explicitly connected to Phillips’s own inability to finance Diasense. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0124] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0416]

- **In:** financing calibrated to actual company needs, preserving founder ownership and avoiding unnecessary dilution or repeated rounds. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0362] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0223]

- **Conditional:** a capital-intensive opportunity is not inherently disqualifying, but it creates financing risk and must fit the company structure and fund model. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0072] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0073]

- **Upside trade-off:** Phillips acknowledges that lower-capital companies may have lower maximum upside, but the fund seeks a higher success rate and moderate exits rather than depending solely on rare unicorns. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0060] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0061]

## deal_terms_valuation

Entry-price discipline is central. Phillips dislikes excessive early-stage valuations because they compress prospective returns, increase future-financing risk, and make moderate exits unacceptable. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0121] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0254] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0222]

- **Fund II profile:** sources describe $1 million–$1.5 million checks, $2 million–$3 million rounds, and roughly $10 million–$15 million pre-money valuations. Other later descriptions use $15 million–$20 million or $15 million–$25 million post-money; these are overlapping snapshots, not one exact universal cap. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0127] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0128] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0248] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0160]

- **Evolution:** An early account reports typical $250,000–$300,000 seed checks. While Fund II was being raised, the same account states a below-$8 million pre-money target; later Fund II accounts describe roughly $1 million–$1.5 million checks. The early Fund II valuation target should not be assigned to Fund I. [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0065] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0069] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0127]

- **Ownership:** sources describe targets around 5–7% plus pro rata, while another interview says 5–10%; the fund’s size prevents a routine 20% target. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0103] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0104] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0180]

- **Return underwriting:** Phillips requires a credible path to about 10x valuation growth. Reported target outcomes range from about $50 million when sufficient for a 3–3.5x cash return, through modeled $100–$150 million exits, to a broader $100–$300 million band; the consistent principle is fund-return fit without requiring unicorn scale. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0099] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0219] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0250]

- **Structure:** he considers tax, QSBS, liquidity, liability, and founder-ownership implications from the beginning. He does not require a named acquirer at seed, but he does begin considering eventual outcomes when investing. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0012] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0013] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0105]

- **Governance evolution:** earlier sources describe minority investing and syndication; later first-party reporting says 11 Tribes shifted toward leading rounds and taking board influence. An aggregator’s statement that the fund does not lead conflicts with that later primary-source account and should not control the memory. [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0016] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0115] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0138] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0325]

## timing

Phillips generally avoids claiming that he can predict markets; in uncertainty he favors patient, opportunistic deployment. He nevertheless described particular vintages as attractive and expects disciplined pricing to regain importance. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0289] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0247] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0350]

- **2021 posture:** post-pandemic themes emphasized distributed work and related technological shifts. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0195] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0167]

- **2024–2026 posture:** vertical AI became an active focus, especially capital-efficient domain applications that may become acquisition targets, while generic wrappers and frontier-model economics remained unattractive. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0165] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0330] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0406]

- **Timing test:** anticipate customer and technology shifts, but do not pay a fashionable price merely because a sector is timely. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0114] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0290]

## investor_fit_constraints

- **Stage:** seed and sometimes Series A, with Fund II predominantly post-revenue; institutional capital is generally unavailable for an idea-only company, though angels may be appropriate. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0209] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0089] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0227]

- **Geography:** current evidence favors North American, non-coastal tier-two and tier-three ecosystems; earlier reporting included one Mexico investment. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0047] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0025]

- **Sector:** broadly generalist, provided capital efficiency and price fit; active interests evolved from future of work, health, data, cybersecurity, food/agtech, and sustainability toward vertical AI and blue-collar niches. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0050] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0074] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0192]

- **Explicit exclusions:** alcohol, marijuana, and other businesses categorized as vice industries; generic AI wrappers and underlying LLM companies are also excluded under the stated Fund II posture. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0391] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0406]

- **Not a religious eligibility screen:** the principles are faith-inspired, but founders need not share a religious affiliation. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0380] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0381]

- **Vision alignment:** the fund may abandon a deal when a founder insists on a heavily funded billion-dollar trajectory, rejects the firm’s founder-resilience partnership, or conflicts materially with the lead investor’s underwriting. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0137] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0172] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0169]

- **Portfolio construction:** sector concentration can block another investment even without direct company conflict. Sources variously describe concentrated portfolios of 20–25 and Fund II plans for 25–30 companies, reflecting different vehicles or points in time. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0102] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0323] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0127]

- **Decision process:** an early source says a four-person committee required unanimity; a later source says the committee had three partners. These may reflect process evolution or differing membership descriptions; the exact timing is unresolved. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0390] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0152]

- **Partnership model:** 11 Tribes allocates an additional non-dilutive amount equal to 2% of each investment for coaching, counseling, leadership development, therapy, or organizational health, and seeks hands-on governance and operating support. [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0037] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0096] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0199]

- **Founder choice matters:** Phillips values founders who choose aligned partners rather than simply maximizing valuation, and he treats the founder as the venture firm’s customer. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0183] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0118]

## Distinctive / doesn't-fit-the-taxonomy

Phillips’s most distinctive feature is the attempt to make whole-person founder support an underwritten portfolio-risk mechanism. The firm does not merely praise resilience: it contractually reserves capital for it and expects coaching, counseling, leadership development, and organizational-health work to reduce founder and co-founder failure modes. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0158] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0364]

His personal failed-founder experience is causally important to that design. Diasense was reportedly overtaken by a competing product, lost expected financing, ran out of cash, and shut down; Phillips explicitly says that failure motivated the firm. [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0006] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0007] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0274]

The strategy also rejects a standard power-law caricature. He still wants scalable companies and approximately 10x investment potential, but tries to construct a small fund around a larger number of plausible sub-unicorn exits, higher survival, restrained dilution, and a broader buyer universe. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0249] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0322] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0331]

Finally, he explicitly acknowledges that venture judgment rests on imperfect patterns, biases, instinct, and learned experience rather than certainty. The policy above should therefore be treated as a probabilistic memory of recurring preferences, explicit constraints, and fund-era evolution—not an automatic scoring system. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0083] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0367]
