# Portfolio and Constraints

## Fund mandate

- **Firm:** Hustle Fund
- **Stage:** Pre-seed / earliest-stage software
- **Observed check range in project metadata:** $5K–$250K
- **Recorded interests:** social, subscription, marketplaces, CPG, fashion, and underestimated founders
- **Geography:** global orientation, including active interest outside Silicon Valley

The talk corpus describes Hustle Fund's institutional first-check model evolving from approximately $25K to approximately $150K. The broader $5K–$250K range above comes from observed project investment metadata and may include personal or context-specific checks; it should not be treated as a standing quote for every Hustle Fund deal.

## Decision constraints

- A small pre-seed check favors companies that can learn, generate revenue, or reach another durable milestone without depending on continuous large financings.
- Capital-intensive and highly crowded categories can be poor fund fits even when the underlying company may be viable.
- Elizabeth's strongest operating bias is toward customer acquisition and higher-LTV “deer-hunting” models. She distinguishes lack of personal model knowledge from a universal judgment that the business is bad.
- Thesis alignment is decisive before product-market fit can be proven.
- Existing holdings should be checked for direct conflicts before engaging; holdings themselves are supplied through the separate chronological portfolio memory.
- Hustle Fund's mission is to broaden access to capital, knowledge, and networks; cold and geographically distributed founders are within scope.
