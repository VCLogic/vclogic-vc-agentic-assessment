# Jenny Fielding — Investment Memory

Jenny Fielding is the co-founder and Managing Partner of Everywhere Ventures, formerly The Fund. This memory synthesizes her source-attributed comments across roles as founder, angel, BBC corporate-venture operator, Techstars managing director, and institutional pre-seed investor. The sources span different periods and often lack publication dates; apparently current policies are therefore only current as of their respective sources. Portfolio counts and mandate descriptions also vary with time and scope. [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1037] [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1109] [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1148]

## founder_team

Fielding’s pre-seed underwriting begins with people because product-market-fit data are commonly unavailable and the initial product may change substantially. She seeks founders who can articulate a distinctive future, explain their right to build it, and navigate toward it as facts change. Exceptional founder conviction can outweigh uncertainty about the present product or market, but it does not excuse weak character or incomplete founder diligence. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1542] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1560] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0993] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1679]

Her recurring positive pattern combines vision, resilience, authentic motivation, learning speed, and decisive execution. She looks for evidence that founders have handled adversity, can interpret limited data quickly, and will change tactics when contrary evidence appears. Persistence is valuable, but repeatedly following a failing strategy is not. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0446] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0544] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1322] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1337]

A personal or professional connection to the problem is a strong preference, especially in healthcare and mental wellness. It can produce empathy, differentiated insight, and durable commitment; excessive emotional attachment becomes a liability when it prevents adaptation. She reports weaker personal fit with founders who simply identify researched white space without genuine problem affinity, although this is a preference rather than a categorical exclusion. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1588] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1590] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1591] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1053]

She evaluates founders longitudinally through several references, local reputation, repeated interactions, conduct in legal and financing negotiations, responses to setbacks, and willingness to address uncomfortable cofounder or equity issues. One exciting meeting can produce false confidence. Honest uncertainty is preferable to an improvised or evasive answer. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0448] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0449] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1001] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1002] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1173]

Character is a real boundary. She favors community-minded, prosocial founders and will decline financially attractive opportunities over dishonesty, disreputable behavior, or an intolerable long-term working relationship. Elite credentials are not sufficient proxies for quality; demonstrated execution and trusted operator references are more informative. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0460] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0954] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1239] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1512] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0171]

Her view of solo founders evolved through experience. She no longer treats solo status as a major disadvantage when the founder has a strong foundational team, complementary talent, meaningful equity incentives, and a credible support system. In the cited source, she describes a company still operated by only one person as probably outside the firm’s fit. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0255] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0256] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0025] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0026]

Her team lens also examines cofounder trust, shared vision, equity allocation, and recruiting ability. She favors CEOs who can align people behind a future and recruit excellent talent through mission and meaningful ownership, while treating unresolved interpersonal instability as a major early-stage risk. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0011] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0012] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0013] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1176] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1462]

Tentative diligence questions supported by her process are: What does the founder believe the market will look like in five to ten years, and what experience or data supports that view? What sustains the founder’s commitment? How did the team handle its hardest financing, personnel, or cofounder problem? What changed after adverse customer data? Why were equity allocations chosen? Can this CEO recruit a team around a shared vision? [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0512] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0005] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0008] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0012] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0013]

## market_opportunity

Fielding wants consequential problems and venture-scale potential, but she does not require a large, measurable market to exist today. A presently small or unknowable market can qualify when the founder offers a credible structural-change thesis, a focused beachhead, and expansion into a much broader category. Unsupported trillion-dollar TAM claims are unpersuasive; nuanced market reasoning matters more. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0304] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0062] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0501] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0499]

Her geographic thesis is explicit: strong founders and innovation are globally distributed, so sourcing should not be restricted to Silicon Valley or New York. Credible global investing nevertheless requires local relationships, market knowledge, reference networks, and support capability rather than a global label alone. Historically reported allocations remained North America-heavy, with approximately 30% international exposure in some accounts. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1216] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1217] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1218] [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0911]

She has expressed particular enthusiasm for Brazil, Mexico, Nigeria, and other African and Latin American ecosystems. Retreating US capital after the 2020–2021 influx made Mexico and Brazil more attractive to her, even though that retreat made fundraising harder for founders. She associates emerging-market opportunity with large underlying markets, resilient entrepreneurs, advancing technology adoption, and sometimes less obstructive legacy infrastructure. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0002] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0003] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1539] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0360]

The long-running thematic frame is the foundational or “table stakes” economy: money, health, and work. Other sources broaden it to resilience-enabling software and infrastructure across healthcare, fintech, future of work, climate, logistics, manufacturing, automation, and selected SaaS. The record shows evolution rather than one immutable list: the firm is variously called generalist, focused on three verticals, or broadly focused on institutional resilience. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0269] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0384] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1352] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1561]

A likely In set is application software and enabling infrastructure that modernizes important, antiquated industries; vertical SaaS; fintech infrastructure; digital-health infrastructure rather than direct care delivery; work automation; climate-enabling technology; and AI that materially transforms an outcome. Reported Out or low-fit areas have included foundational AI infrastructure, direct healthcare delivery, consumer social, consumer products, CPG, biotech, hardware, and oversized seed rounds. These boundaries have varied by period, and Techstars or personal-investment activity creates exceptions. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0125] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1593] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0663] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1236]

## competition_defensibility

Fielding increasingly evaluates defensibility over a five-to-ten-year horizon, especially as foundation models advance. She asks whether a company remains relevant as model costs fall, quality improves, and incumbent or open-source alternatives expand. She acknowledges limited confidence in predicting moats throughout a 10–15-year venture cycle, making founder adaptability and portfolio diversification part of the response. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0247] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0392] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1360] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1371]

Credible moat candidates include distribution, difficult integrations, proprietary source material, regulatory capability, embedded workflows, pipeline, and speed to market. Data alone may be insufficient, and generic generative-AI functionality is weak without distinctive inputs or distribution. She expects go-to-market strategy to reinforce defensibility rather than exist as an unrelated plan. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0094] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1365] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1367] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1368] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1083]

She describes workflow automation, mental-health applications, security applications, creator tools, and some AI wrappers as crowded. Crowding is not itself a rejection, but it increases the burden to explain novelty, adoption, retention, distribution, and why an incumbent or foundation-model provider will not absorb the feature. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0030] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0892] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0773] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0889]

Inferred, tentative defensibility questions include: What survives if the underlying model becomes cheaper and better? Does the company own distribution, difficult integrations, proprietary inputs, regulatory progress, or an embedded workflow? Why can established alternatives not serve the customer? Is early demand organic and retained, or driven by novelty? [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0525] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0579] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0650] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0652]

## product_solution

She starts with the customer problem, not a technology label. A pitch should establish the deficient status quo, affected user, acute pain, and concrete product before implementation detail. Must-have pain generates an immediate response; jargon, vague “all-in-one” language, or presenting the solution before establishing the pain weakens conviction. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1663] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0331] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0318] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0319]

At pre-seed she increasingly prefers something tangible—a prototype or demonstration—particularly from technical founders, without requiring polish or revenue. The purpose is to show that the team builds and executes rather than merely describing an idea. For hard tech, outsourcing the core technical capability is a warning, and technology risk must be evaluated alongside normal business-model risk. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0385] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1285] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1287] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0041] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0045]

AI is an enabler, not a standalone thesis. Fielding asks whether it is genuinely appropriate, materially improves the outcome, and makes a business or institution more efficient or resilient. Failure to use contemporary AI tools internally can be an execution warning, but simply using AI does not make an operating business an “AI company.” [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1594] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1595] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0027] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0146]

Adoption is distinct from technical feasibility. She probes whether customers will add another tool, whether elderly or other intended users can navigate it, whether government or enterprise stakeholders must participate, and whether the product can integrate into channels customers already use. Waitlists, pilots, customer discussions, built products, and organic inbound demand are useful early signals, but not universal prerequisites. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0750] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0581] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0772] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1178]

## traction_growth

Fielding separates pre-seed momentum from later-stage traction. A pre-seed company may be pre-revenue, but she wants continuing progress in product, customer conversations, pilots, hiring, or financing. The founder should keep executing whether or not the current round closes. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1290] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1340] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1341]

A subsequent seed round requires more than vision. Her market observations suggest that a typical vertical-AI or SaaS company should use pre-seed capital to reach several hundred thousand dollars of ARR and strong momentum; she has also described revenue approaching $1 million ARR as a strong seed signal. These are contextual observations, not fixed Everywhere investment gates. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0140] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0141] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0018]

For Series A, she reports a reset from the historical $1 million ARR shorthand toward roughly $2–3 million, with growth velocity, pipeline, retention, team, and vision all relevant. Reaching a revenue figure slowly may be weaker than reaching it quickly, and no single number substitutes for repeatable progress. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0959] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0960] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0961] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0396]

Fundraising speed and valuation markups do not validate the business. Closely spaced rounds can obscure retention, while repeated requests for one additional customer, pilot, or metric may signal a soft rejection rather than a genuine diligence threshold. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0521] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0524] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0558]

## business_model_economics

She wants sound unit economics, command of financial drivers, and a credible path to sustainability, while recognizing that immediate profitability is not expected at pre-seed. Founders should build their own models, understand the assumptions, and connect the financing ask to operating milestones. Venture capital should accelerate a customer proposition that works rather than permanently subsidize weak demand. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0184] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0489] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0497] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1460]

Capital efficiency is a persistent preference. She questions oversized early engineering teams, heavy paid acquisition, and plans dependent on serial financing. She considers approximately 18–24 months of runway conventional while rejecting both unnecessary excess and raises too small to finance the next stage. These are financing-risk judgments, not blanket refusals to support ambitious companies. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0912] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0226] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1194] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1196]

For consumer businesses, customer acquisition is the foremost question: she wants differentiated, capital-efficient channels and concrete CAC logic rather than generic organic-growth claims or a list of tactics. In healthcare and other regulated markets, payer incentives, sales cycles, integration, regulation, and stakeholder behavior are integral to the model. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0075] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0491] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0492] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0860] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0887]

Venture fit is not synonymous with business quality. She distinguishes scalable, high-growth companies capable of fund-level returns from attractive small, service, or slower-growth businesses better matched with other capital. She also appreciates bootstrapping and models able to alternate between self-financing and external funding. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1666] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1667] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1201] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1203]

## deal_terms_valuation

Everywhere’s core mandate is the first institutional pre-seed financing, generally a $500,000–$2 million round, with checks commonly around $250,000 and smaller scout checks in some structures. The firm can lead or follow, prefers collaboration, and historically made one core-fund check, using SPVs rather than conventional reserves for selected follow-ons. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0474] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0688] [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0258] [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0079]

Valuation discipline follows from small checks, small ownership positions, and limited direct follow-on capital. Fielding has cited a roughly $10 million pre-seed ceiling and elsewhere a typical $6–12 million market range, excluding unusually competitive rounds. She tolerates modest differences or may find a way into a genuine outlier, but $20–30 million or $25 million pre-seed pricing generally does not fit the model. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0475] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1193] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0997] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1380]

She evaluates price relative to plausible exit value and next-round requirements. High early valuations unsupported by product, revenue, or attainable milestones can create financing risk, and she rejects assumptions that valuations can reliably increase three-to-fivefold each round. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0476] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0194] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0195]

Cap-table diligence is substantial. She prefers founders to retain majority ownership before financing, generally favors four-year vesting, wants an option pool capable of supporting recruitment, and treats stacked instruments, tiny pools, large fully vested advisor grants, or founder dilution beyond 50% by seed as serious concerns. These are specific preferences rather than universal legal prescriptions. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1600] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1601] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1604] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1609] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1611]

## timing

Her default horizon is approximately ten years. She favors structural change over current fashion and wants founders to explain both why now and why the company will remain relevant later. Customer readiness is central: a valid future use case can still fail if infrastructure, psychology, or institutional behavior is not ready. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0427] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1362] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1596] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1598]

Fielding is contrarian across cycles. She has described downturns and post-crisis vintages as attractive, cited cooled fintech sentiment and lower pricing as reasons to invest, and prefers maintaining activity through unpopular periods rather than entering only when markets are crowded. This coexists with tactical advice to raise when demand is favorable and conserve runway when it is not. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0221] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0191] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1104] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1624]

Themes have evolved with conditions. COVID increased her attention to remote work, employee engagement, digital health, therapeutics, mental health, and insurance digitization; later evidence emphasizes application-layer AI, legacy-industry automation, and AI-enabled healthcare. Technology labels never substitute for real customer problems. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0709] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0712] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0218] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0736] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0814]

## investor_fit_constraints

Everywhere is optimized for very early, collaborative, relatively small rounds—not $5 million seed rounds, most consumer startups under the cited mandate, or companies already priced as mature winners. The firm seeks opportunities where Fielding believes it can provide meaningful help and maintain a productive long-term founder relationship. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0016] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0055] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0060]

Its value-add is a many-to-many network of founder and operator LPs used for sourcing, references, specialist diligence, customer and investor introductions, and portfolio support. Fielding’s role is active but usually informal: the firm generally does not take board seats and aims to help companies become ready for seed or a priced round rather than promising indefinite direct support. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0105] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0821] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1347] [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0834]

Capacity is a practical boundary. A high-volume portfolio requires support to scale through community systems; direct investing occupied only about 20–25% of Fielding’s time in some accounts, and cold-inbound volume makes detailed review difficult. Trusted referrals and thoughtful personalized outreach therefore have practical advantages without being formal requirements. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1065] [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0266] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0926] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1585]

The fund is conviction-driven rather than consensus-driven: one partner may champion an investment, while either partner can veto over character or community-stewardship concerns. Participation by a respected peer is favorable but not decisive; an unusually early optionality check from a mega-fund may instead create later signaling risk. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0479] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1239] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0170] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0702]

Inferred and tentative, a fit checklist would cover stage and thesis fit; plausible fund-returning upside; exceptional and ethical founders; distinctive market insight; a genuine must-have problem; tangible execution; durable distribution, integration, regulatory, or workflow advantages; capital efficiency; healthy equity incentives; and a credible path to independently financeable milestones. It is a synthesis, not a mechanical scorecard or a set of universal gates. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0941] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0946] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0664] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1644]

## Distinctive / doesn't-fit-the-taxonomy

Fielding’s institutional model is unusually high-volume: approximately 75–90 investments per fund, small checks, low entry prices, acceptance of a high loss rate, and a test that each prospective winner could return the fund. This is paired with selective secondary liquidity and SPVs for mature winners rather than conventional ownership-maximizing reserves. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0929] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0932] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0470] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0467]

Her framework contains a productive tension: Everywhere is variously described as generalist, money-health-work focused, resilience-economy focused, and software-only. The most consistent reading is a broad pre-seed search constrained by stage, fund economics, founder quality, and an enabling-software orientation, with themes evolving through portfolio learning rather than fashion. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0550] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0551] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0662] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1492]

Personal angel activity is more flexible than institutional investing. Fielding may personally back compelling, mission-aligned founders outside the fund thesis, and personal angel outcomes can work at exit values too small for a venture fund. This exception should not be mistaken for Everywhere’s mandate. [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0680] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-1099] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0836] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0837]
