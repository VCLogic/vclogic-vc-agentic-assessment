## Fund allocation and construction

- **Explicit — power-law portfolio:** He expects most startups not to return capital, so each selection needs outlier potential; historical portfolio models ranged from roughly 30–50 initial investments, with a smaller set of follow-ons. [ev:mac-conwell-1468] [ev:mac-conwell-1469] [ctx:mac-conwell-1474] [ctx:mac-conwell-1475]

- **Explicit — larger early checks, selective follow-ons:** The strategy seeks meaningful initial ownership exposure very early while reserving larger follow-on capital for emerging winners; later statements cap one-company exposure at 5% of the fund and $500,000. [ev:mac-conwell-0626] [ev:mac-conwell-1399] [ev:mac-conwell-1433]

- **Explicit — returns over mechanical ownership:** He evaluates prospective return multiples rather than enforcing a rigid ownership target that could exclude an exceptional company. [ev:mac-conwell-0629] [ev:mac-conwell-0631]

- **Explicit — LP fairness:** He preferred a traditional fund in which all LPs share exposure to investments across the fund’s life and rejected rolling-fund cohort economics that could exclude early supporters from later winners. [ev:mac-conwell-0679] [ev:mac-conwell-0680]

- **Explicit — fund access and diversification:** He supports lower LP minimums and higher permitted investor counts because smaller commitments let more investors diversify across funds; he describes regulatory investor-count limits as a constraint on emerging managers. [ev:mac-conwell-0371] [ev:mac-conwell-0373] [ev:mac-conwell-1451]

- **Evolution:** Fund I was later described as undercapitalized for the desired small-initial-check-plus-follow-on strategy, while the investment thesis itself remained stable between Funds I and II. [ev:mac-conwell-1425] [ev:mac-conwell-1426] [ev:mac-conwell-1427]
