# Jake Chapman — Investment Memory

This memory resolves to Jake Chapman of Marque Ventures, not any same-name person. It synthesizes attributed speech and clearly attributed quotations with source-reported context. Chapman’s views span several institutional eras—“Saze Partners” (an unverified transcript rendering), Gelt Venture Capital, Alpha Bridge Ventures, Army Venture Capital Corporation, and Marque Ventures—so an older statement should not automatically be treated as Marque’s current mandate. Many transcripts lack publication dates; current-sounding claims are therefore only as current as their unidentified source dates. Several recordings lacked transcripts, repeated pages may derive from the same interview, and source-reported relationships are not independent verification.

## founder_team

Chapman treats team quality as a central underwriting variable, not a soft supplement to product diligence. His stated “biggest learning” is that team is critical, while his experience suggests many post-seed failures arise from human and organizational problems rather than technology or product-market fit. The resulting diligence asks whether the CEO can mature from inventor into organizational leader, make sound decisions, remain resilient, recruit effectively, and continue inspiring the team as the company scales. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0019] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0020] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0151] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0152] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0372]

He favors unusually ambitious founders pursuing consequential outcomes. He looks for an audacious goal that he and his team also want to exist, and says founders must genuinely love the product or problem because they will repeatedly have to choose the company through existential difficulty. Visible indifference is a negative signal; durable commitment, including commitment demonstrated during downturns, is positive. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0139] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0140] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0186] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0315] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0316] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0354]

Thoughtfulness matters more than agreement. Chapman says his team is most impressed when a founder gives a deeply considered answer to a hard question, even if the conclusion differs from the investor’s. He similarly wants market estimates explained through the founder’s reasoning and understanding of actual customers, not defended as a single supposedly authoritative number. Tailored outreach, an immediately intelligible value proposition, and resourceful relationship-building can help reveal preparation and initiative. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0187] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0272] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0273] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0175] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0413]

For defense, founder-market fit is unusually important. He biases toward veterans or former government civilians who personally experienced the problem, while expressly allowing that outsiders can still succeed. At minimum, he wants a co-founder or very early hire with firsthand military, acquisition, and bureaucratic experience; he describes defense as difficult to enter with only a bright idea and strong technology. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0129] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0180] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0281] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0308] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0309]

Team-specific warning signs include severe integrity or behavioral risk, an entirely novice organization, and unresolved leadership structure. He has stopped diligence over extreme team risk, considers trustworthiness essential to decade-long venture relationships, and views co-CEO arrangements as evidence that founders may have avoided or failed to resolve a difficult authority question. The novice-team concern is inferred from operating advice rather than stated as a universal investment prohibition. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0205] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0366] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0258] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0384]

Chapman evaluates people over repeated interactions, including informal settings, and conducts reference calls throughout diligence, with off-list references reported as especially valuable. He cautions that actual founder-investor working fit may only become fully visible after the check. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0259] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0260] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0387] [ctx:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0232]

## market_opportunity

The opportunity must be large enough to support a venture-scale exit, but Chapman cares more about a defensible bottom-up derivation than precision theater. One of his favored questions is what the business becomes in ten or twenty years if everything works; he wants the ambitious end state first, followed by the credible path to reach it. Market weakness is one of his most common red flags, and a merely good low-nine-figure business may still fail his fund’s return requirements. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0262] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0263] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0271] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0381] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0382]

In defense, he does not model the Department of Defense as one customer: he describes thousands of offices across services and agencies, with allied defense markets expanding the addressable opportunity beyond the United States. His demand analysis nevertheless separates end-user enthusiasm from formal requirements, budgets, contracting authority, and congressional support. A warfighter who likes a product may have neither money nor purchasing authority. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0051] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0300] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0009] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0011] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0286]

His strongest present opportunity areas are national-security-relevant deep technology and the defense industrial base: advanced and domestic manufacturing, supply-chain security, critical minerals, alternative positioning/navigation/timing, kinetics and energetics, aerospace, autonomy, robotics, applied AI, synthetic biology, and hardware-software combinations. Manufacturing is especially prominent because he sees reshoring and friend-shoring as a multidecade, potentially trillion-dollar undertaking and perceives a missing neutral production partner able to serve multiple defense startups. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0002] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0003] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0039] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0056] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0057] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0124] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0254] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0253]

He sees tactical categories as unevenly crowded. Small drones are the clearest “Out/very high bar”: he says the market has far more companies than needed and warns against underwriting a use case whose primary demand is one current conflict. Strategic technologies and mobilization-ready industrial inputs appear comparatively neglected. This is category skepticism, not a universal ban: his Firestorm investment followed recognition that expeditionary manufacturing differentiated the company from crowded small-UAS peers. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0123] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0125] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0292] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0332] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0251] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0274] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0276]

## competition_defensibility

Chapman expects founders to know competitors and explain differentiation without dismissing serious alternatives. Saying there are no competitors, or reducing differentiation to a feature checklist, is a negative signal. His preferred frame is the customer-value difference plus a technological, market, or data mechanism that can create repeatable and sustainable advantage. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0188] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0319] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0415] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0424] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0401]

He is skeptical of fashionable labels without substance. Most companies placing “AI” in a deck do not, in his view, possess compelling proprietary AI; use of a commodity algorithm is not enough. Likewise, prominent military advisers and decorated advisory boards do not substitute for customer access, adoption, or procurement competence. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0083] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0084] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0053] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0055] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0368] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0419]

In defense, specialized knowledge itself is part of the edge. Security clearances institutionalize information asymmetry, and Chapman argues that investors lacking direct contact with Capitol Hill, warfighters, and actual buyers are effectively guessing. Marque reports that its staff hold clearances and operates a fellowship network connecting it to government users and decision-makers. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0128] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0331] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0426] [ctx:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0050] [ctx:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0301]

He sees the procurement system as both moat and distortion. Multiyear budgeting, requirements, red tape, and capital intensity favor incumbents able to survive the process, while government laboratories and incumbent integrators may fail to transition technology efficiently. The investable thesis is that expert navigation can generate alpha while these frictions persist, although successful reform would reduce that edge. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0120] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0162] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0164] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0247] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0130] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0427]

## product_solution

Chapman wants a product anchored to an identifiable problem and actual user behavior. Cool technology can remain a solution in search of a problem or become unusable because its builders missed operational details. Marque generally seeks at least a prototype placed in users’ hands and directly checks who is using it, which ordinarily pushes the firm later than pre-seed. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0235] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0181] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0182] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0127]

For defense products, formal requirement recognition is central to scalable purchasing. The diligence questions are: Is there a requirement now? If not, where will it come from? Who is the end user, who owns the budget, who writes the requirement, and who controls the contract? End-user validation is strong evidence but not equivalent to procurement readiness. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0007] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0008] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0033] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0286]

He favors complete customer solutions rather than isolated components because DoD buys integrated capability. Software can unlock multiple capabilities from limited hardware platforms, but connected systems also expand attack surfaces and therefore require cybersecurity alongside sensing and connectivity. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0044] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0161] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0165]

His AI interest is application-led. He sees compelling value where AI automates expensive compliance or compresses months-long defense workflows into weeks, while still asking whether the technology is genuinely differentiated and whether inference economics can work. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0005] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0029] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0083] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0192]

Inferred product-selection rule: begin with one customer who urgently wants the product, make it work at scale, and expand the core technology into adjacent markets only after establishing that wedge. This comes from operating advice and examples, not a universal stated prerequisite. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0050] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0122] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0126]

## traction_growth

Chapman privileges evidence of execution over plans. A working product, meaningful revenue, product-market or warfighter fit, and conversion from pilots to commercial deployment carry more weight than promises. In defense, grants, one contract, and verbal interest are not by themselves proof of repeatable traction; R&D awards often fail to lead to procurement. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0359] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0196] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0418] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0330] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0031]

His defense growth model requires concurrent work on product, acquisition, requirements, budgets, and congressional education. Procurement officials should know the company and ideally place it into future plans while R&D proceeds. Partnering with an incumbent prime or ManTech-like contractor can offer an earlier revenue path, customer interaction, and feedback, but Chapman does not confuse this route with proof of eventual scaled procurement. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0032] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0035] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0336] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0284] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0285]

Outside defense, he looks for repeat behavior, credible retention, organic demand, and efficient acquisition. A jointly authored pass cited sub-10% repeat purchasing, untested recurring-revenue ideas, weak brand connection, and dependence on paid digital acquisition as reasons not to invest. Those figures belong to one consumer-company diligence case and should not be generalized into universal gates. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0375] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0377] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0378] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0379]

He recognizes that venture capital changes the operating objective: companies take on pressure for rapid, repeated growth and further financings rather than simply pursuing steady profitability. Founders should therefore demonstrate milestones capable of supporting the next round, not merely near-term survival. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0065] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0066] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0025]

## business_model_economics

Chapman underwrites venture through a power-law lens: outlier successes drive returns, and a successful fund may require a 50x–100x company. In one Alpha Bridge rejection, the stated model required each opportunity to be underwritable to a potential 15x–20x net return; elsewhere he described a small-fund construction in which modest ownership of a $1 billion-plus exit could return the fund, and several moderate exits or one unicorn could target 3x net to LPs. These are fund-model examples, not universal company hurdles. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0060] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0061] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0382] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0408] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0409]

Hardware diligence should expose bill-of-materials costs and gross margins when upfront equipment sales dominate lifetime value. More broadly, he wants a clear monetization path from pilots and partnerships, accepts that an early model will evolve, but dislikes a scattered set of monetization experiments with no coherent initial focus. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0360] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0361] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0416] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0417]

Defense hard tech is explicitly capital intensive. Chapman expects multiple rounds and large checks, sometimes tens or hundreds of millions before program-of-record or scaled government purchasing. Because Marque may fund a Series A but not a Series B, capital risk is part of diligence: can this team and milestone plan attract the next financing when needed? This is a financing-risk warning, not a blanket refusal to invest in capital-intensive companies. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0021] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0022] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0024] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0025] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0290]

He rejects the economics of traditional cost-plus contracting for venture-backed startups because it rewards higher costs and commonly produces margins inconsistent with venture returns. He instead favors fixed-price structures that let suppliers improve margins by lowering costs while delivering capability. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0248] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0249] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0250] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0337]

Chapman is comparatively positive on consumer subscriptions, usage-based payment for sufficiently valuable AI, and business models aligning customer economics with adoption. He has highlighted healthcare products where providers preserve reimbursement while reducing visits and patients receive convenience, data, education, and access. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0093] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0193] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0237] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0238]

## deal_terms_valuation

Price is inseparable from quality: Chapman’s concise rule is that every company can be a good investment at the right price. He worries that hot defense companies may not grow into elevated valuations if category enthusiasm fades, but he also accepts a seemingly high valuation where plausible fund-returning upside overwhelms the limited impact of a loss for a sufficiently large fund. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0398] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0329] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0087]

He is comparatively tolerant of founder secondary liquidity. He supports enough liquidity or compensation to remove basic family financial stress while preserving motivation, but notes that secondaries are uncommon in a first round or Series A and become more normal around Series B or C. This is a calibration judgment, not an endorsement of unrestricted early liquidity. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0088] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0089] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0090] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0441] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0442]

He warns that milestone or tranche structures can backfire by making the later, de-risked investment more attractive than the proposed early entry. He also regards legal documents primarily as expectation-setting instruments; long-run conduct is ultimately governed by relationships and trust. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0362] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0261] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0366]

## timing

Chapman’s overarching rule is to invest in the world expected two, three, or five years ahead, not in today’s hottest category or the technology most visible in a current war. In defense, he uses congressional budgets and multiyear priorities to map future spending, while warning that present battlefield evidence—especially Ukraine-centered demand—should not be mechanically extrapolated into the next conflict. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0017] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0018] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0177] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0178] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0291] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0306]

He sees defense as early in a generational transition. Persistent security commitments, pressure to diversify the industrial base, and a prospective shift of budgets from costly primes toward startups support the thesis; specialized investors may capture alpha before the market fully understands the opportunity. He simultaneously acknowledges self-interest in calling the market “early innings” and says defense’s exit market remains immature. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0006] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0028] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0047] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0118] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0324] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0325] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0327]

The timing thesis contains a financing tension. More capital and founder interest make defense companies increasingly viable, but also inflate scarce scaled assets and attract investors lacking disciplined hardware or DoD processes. Chapman has predicted both a Series A funding crunch and eventual retreat by investors who get burned. The attractive entry is therefore contrarian but financeable: see the “magic” before consensus, while believing later capital will recognize it by the next round. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0023] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0041] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0076] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0077] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0078] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0116]

Earlier, broader comments show that his timing framework is not defense-only: during the 2020 crisis he expected lower entry prices, later-cycle exits, stronger founder selection, concentrated talent, lower customer-acquisition costs in some sectors, and durable behavioral changes. Those observations were explicitly crisis-period views, not timeless forecasts. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0338] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0341] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0353] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0354] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0355] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0356]

## investor_fit_constraints

Current Marque fit is early-stage—principally seed through Series A—and almost exclusively technology relevant to national security, usually serving government needs and intended for the United States and allies. The firm can lead or follow. An earlier Marque fund was described as pre-seed through Series A, while a proposed successor would move mostly to Series A with some seed; the precise current mix is source-date dependent. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0037] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0040] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0278] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0279] [ctx:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0246] [ctx:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0247]

Selective In preferences are pure-play defense; advanced manufacturing; strategic and kinetic systems; energetics; aerospace, autonomy, robotics, applied AI, cyber, space, and other frontier technologies; hardware-software combinations; products with real warfighter demand and a credible requirements/budget path; founders with firsthand domain knowledge; and missions Chapman wants to exist. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0038] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0039] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0157] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0167] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0289] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0391] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0429]

Selective Out or high-bar areas are unethical intended uses or dubious customers; investment activity aligned against U.S. interests, including his stated opposition to investing in China; crowded undifferentiated small drones; commodity-AI claims; products without identifiable demand; ad tech based on his founder experience; and, in an older profile, purely consumer-facing, e-commerce, game-studio, and ad-tech businesses. These boundaries should be read by era: Alpha Bridge itself had consumer exposure through another partner, whereas Marque’s later mandate is much narrower. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0132] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0277] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0332] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0434] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0235] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0265] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0430] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0091]

Dual use is a major boundary with nuance. Chapman prefers pure-play defense and calls parallel dual-use execution problematic because different products, engineering teams, and sales motions can exceed seed-stage resources. His preferred pattern is sequential: establish one market, then open the second around Series B. The exception is “dual use of inputs”—industrial capacity designed to switch during mobilization—which he regards as underinvested. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0157] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0158] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0241] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0242] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0243] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0251]

Fit also depends on Marque’s ability to help. Chapman says diligence catalogs every hurdle, tests whether it is surmountable, and distinguishes obstacles the firm can help clear from those it cannot. Marque offers Washington navigation, legislative-affairs leverage, portfolio product synergies, and contracting relationships, but says it cannot manufacture product-market or warfighter fit for a founder. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0043] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0136] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0287] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0288] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0289] [ctx:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0128]

A tentative, inferred diligence sequence is: verify ethical intent and mandate fit; identify the user, requirement, budget owner, contracting path, and congressional dependencies; test prototype use and differentiation; assess founder thoughtfulness, resilience, domain access, and trustworthiness; model capital needed through the next institutional round; and confirm that the outcome can support venture-scale returns. This synthesis combines explicit questions and operating advice and is not a published universal checklist. ## Distinctive / doesn't-fit-the-taxonomy

Chapman’s most distinctive through-line is moral allocation: venture capital is a high-leverage way to shape the future, and he frames each investment as a vote for the world he wants for his family. Personal passion is not merely branding; he says a GP who is not deeply enamored of a company is probably the wrong investor, and he seeks the combination of meaningful impact and large financial upside. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0001] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0145] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0391] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0394] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0395] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0399] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0404] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0405]

A second distinctive element is the evolution from Alpha Bridge’s “healthy founders, healthy returns” model to Marque’s defense-specialist platform. Alpha Bridge paid for customized coaching, wellness, and personal-support programs because Chapman believed founder resilience improved company outcomes and helped a small fund preserve follow-on access. Marque applies the same non-commodity-capital instinct through domain access, government navigation, fellowships, legislative support, and strategic partnerships. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0150] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0153] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0156] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0204] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0222] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0136] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0410]

Finally, he is unusually explicit about his own incentives and limits: venture capital biases him toward seeing finance as a solution; small funds cannot backstop every later round; investor rejections often reflect portfolio conflicts or internal firm allocation rather than company quality; and his own expertise does not extend to debt or LBO markets.
